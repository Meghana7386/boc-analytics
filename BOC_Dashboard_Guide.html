Executive Dashboard
🎯 Purpose

This is the first thing you see when you open the dashboard. It gives a bird's-eye view of your entire procurement operation — total money spent, how many bills, how many vendors, and any active alerts — all in one page. Think of it as your morning briefing.

📊 KPI Cards — The 6 Key Numbers
💸
Total Spend
30.44B
Total money spent across all invoices. Shown in the invoice currency (mostly IDR = Indonesian Rupiah, so "B" = Billion IDR)
🧾
Avg Invoice
3.18M
Average bill amount. If a single invoice is way above this, it's a red flag worth investigating
📋
Total Invoices
9,723
Total number of bills processed. This is your data volume — everything else is calculated from these records
🏪
Unique Vendors
3,602
Number of distinct suppliers paid. High number = diverse supply chain. Low number = risky dependency
🏷️
Categories
12
Spend categories: Food, Travel, Healthcare, Technology, etc. Helps understand where money is going
📊
MoM Growth
+0.0%
Month-over-Month spend change. Positive = spending more than last month. Negative = spending less
📈 Charts & Graphs
📈 Monthly Spend Trend Line + Bar
Purple line = Total spend per month. Teal bars = Number of invoices per month. Two Y-axes: left for spend amount, right for invoice count. Shows how your spending changed over time month by month.
💡 A sudden spike in the line means a very large bill was paid that month. A spike in bars means many small bills were processed.
🍩 Spend by Category Donut Chart
Shows what percentage of your total money goes to each category. Larger slices = more spend. "Other" being 43.6% means almost half your spend is uncategorized — needs cleanup.
💡 Hover on any slice to see exact % and total amount for that category.
🏆 Top 10 Vendors Horizontal Bar
Shows which 10 vendors received the most money. Bar length = total spend. Number on bar = invoice count from that vendor. If 1–2 vendors dominate, you have a vendor concentration risk.
💡 "Streamrp" being at the top means they received the highest total payment in your dataset.
💱 Currency Distribution Bar Chart
Shows how many invoices exist per currency. IDR (7,163 invoices) dominates because most users are Indonesian. USD has 817. Helps understand your multi-currency exposure.
💡 IDR = Indonesian Rupiah. 1 USD ≈ 16,000 IDR. So "30B IDR" is only ~$1.9M USD — not actually huge.
🎯 Optimization Score Gauge
A score out of 100 for your overall procurement health. 70.7 = Grade B. Based on: vendor concentration, price variances, savings opportunities, and anomaly count.

🟢 80–100 = Excellent (Grade A)
🟡 60–80 = Good (Grade B) ← You are here
🔴 0–60 = Needs improvement (Grade C/D)

Module 2 of 9
Spend Analytics
🎯 Purpose

Goes deeper than the Executive Dashboard. While the Executive Dashboard gives you the totals, Spend Analytics shows you the patterns, trends, and breakdowns of where money is going — by category, vendor, day of week, and how concentrated the spending is. It answers: "HOW are we spending?"

📊 Charts & What They Show
📅 Monthly Spend Trend Dual-Axis Line
More detailed version of the Executive Dashboard trend. Shows Total Spend (purple line, left axis) vs Average Invoice value (teal dotted line, right axis) side-by-side per month. Helps you spot if spend is rising due to more invoices or higher per-invoice amounts.
💡 If total spend goes up but avg invoice stays flat → more bills being submitted. If avg invoice spikes → someone submitted a very large bill.
🏷️ Category-wise Spend Bar Chart
Vertical bar chart showing total spend per category with percentage labels. Each bar = one category (Food, Travel, Healthcare, etc.). Taller bar = more spend in that category. Percentage shown on top of each bar tells you that category's share of total spend.
💡 Use this to identify which categories are your biggest cost drivers and where budget control is needed most.
🗺️ Vendor Spend Treemap Treemap
A rectangle map where each box = one vendor. Bigger box = more money paid to that vendor. Darker purple-to-teal color = higher spend. Shows 30 top vendors at once. Instantly visual — you can see who dominates at a glance.
💡 If one box takes up half the treemap, that vendor holds major financial influence over your procurement.
📅 Spend by Day of Week Bar Chart
Shows which days of the week have the most invoice activity. X-axis = Mon to Sun, Y-axis = total spend. Helps understand procurement behavior patterns — are bills submitted mostly on weekdays? Friday rush? Weekend anomalies?
💡 High weekend spend may indicate unauthorized procurement. High Monday spend = batch processing from previous week.
📊 Spend Concentration (Pareto Chart) Bar + Line
Based on the famous 80/20 rule — in most businesses, 20% of vendors account for 80% of spend. This chart shows bars for each vendor (sorted by spend, highest first) with a cumulative % line. The line hitting 80% early means you have high concentration.

How to read it: Find where the orange line crosses 80%. If it crosses after only 5 vendors = very concentrated. If it crosses after 200 vendors = well diversified.
💡 Your data: Top 5 vendors = 76.4% of spend. This is HIGH concentration — a red flag for procurement risk.
📋 Category Performance Table Data Table
A sortable table showing all categories with their metrics in one place:
• Total Spend — money paid in this category
• Invoice Count — number of bills
• Avg Invoice — average bill size
• Spend % — share of total budget

Sort by any column to find your biggest or most active categories.
💡 Compare "Avg Invoice" across categories — if Technology avg invoice is 10x Food avg invoice, they serve very different needs.

Module 3 of 9
Cost Optimization
🎯 Purpose

This module is your money-saving advisor. It analyzes your invoices to find where you are overpaying, which vendors charge more than market rate, and gives you a list of savings opportunities. It answers: "Where can we cut costs without cutting quality?"

📊 Charts & What They Show
📊 Price Variance Analysis Scatter Plot
Each dot = one vendor. X-axis = number of invoices, Y-axis = price variance (how much they deviate from average). Dots far to the right = high-volume vendors. Dots far up = vendors with large price swings.

Red dots = vendors with both high volume AND high price variance = biggest risk/opportunity.
💡 Focus your negotiation efforts on vendors in the top-right quadrant (high volume + high variance).
🎯 Score Breakdown Horizontal Bars
Breaks down the Optimization Score (70.7/100) into its 4 components:
• Vendor Concentration Score — how diversified are your vendors?
• Price Consistency Score — how stable are vendor prices?
• Savings Capture Rate — how much savings opportunity are you acting on?
• Anomaly Rate Score — how many suspicious invoices exist?
💡 The lowest-scoring component is your biggest opportunity area to improve overall procurement health.
💡 Top Savings Opportunities Bar Chart
Lists specific vendors where switching or negotiating could save money. Each bar shows estimated savings amount if you used the cheaper alternative for that item/category. Sorted from highest savings to lowest.
💡 Start with the longest bar — that's where a single negotiation could save you the most money.
🏪 Vendor Price Comparison by Category Grouped Bar
For each category (Food, Travel, etc.), shows multiple vendors side-by-side with their average price. You can visually see: "For Food & Groceries, Vendor A charges 3x what Vendor B charges for similar items."

This is direct evidence for vendor negotiation or switching decisions.
💡 If two vendors serve the same category but one bar is much taller — you have a clear case to renegotiate.
⚠️ Cost Leakage — Overpriced Vendors Table
A ranked table of vendors who are charging significantly above the category average price. For each vendor it shows:
• How much above average they charge (%)
• Total estimated money lost due to overpaying
• Number of invoices affected

Example: If average Food invoice = ₹500 and a vendor charges ₹5,000 — that's 900% above average = ₹4,500 cost leakage per invoice.
💡 "Cost leakage" = money that left your organization but shouldn't have at that price. This table helps you recover it.

Vendor Analytics
🎯 Purpose

Focuses entirely on your suppliers and vendors. Scores each vendor, classifies them into performance tiers, and gives a full vendor report card. It answers: "Which vendors are reliable, which are expensive, and which should we review?"

📊 Charts & What They Show
📊 Vendor Score Distribution Histogram
A histogram showing how vendor scores are distributed. X-axis = score range (0–100). Y-axis = number of vendors in that score range. A bell curve-shaped histogram means most vendors are average. Left-skewed means most vendors have low scores = problematic supply chain.
💡 Scores are calculated from: price consistency, invoice volume, and pricing relative to category average.
🥧 Classification Mix Pie Chart
Classifies ALL vendors into 4 performance tiers and shows what % fall into each:
🌟 Strategic — High spend, consistent pricing, trusted partners
✅ Preferred — Good performance, moderate spend
⚠️ Review — Inconsistent pricing or low volume
❌ Problematic — High variance, overpriced, or suspicious
💡 Goal: Maximize "Strategic" and "Preferred" slices. Minimize "Problematic" through negotiation or replacement.
🌡️ Vendor Performance Heatmap (Top 30) Heatmap
A grid/matrix showing your top 30 vendors across multiple performance dimensions at once. Each cell is color-coded:
🟢 Green cell = Good performance on that metric
🔴 Red cell = Poor performance on that metric

Rows = Vendors. Columns = metrics (Total Spend, Invoice Count, Avg Price, Price Variance, Score). You can immediately spot: "This vendor scores great on volume but terrible on price consistency."
💡 Look for vendors with mostly red cells — those are your "watch list" vendors that need immediate review or renegotiation.
📋 Full Vendor Scorecard Sortable Table
A comprehensive table listing ALL vendors with their complete metrics. Columns include:
• Vendor Name
• Category — what they sell
• Total Spend — how much you've paid them
• Invoice Count — how many bills from them
• Avg Price — their typical invoice amount
• Price Variance — how consistent their pricing is (low = consistent, high = unpredictable)
• Score — overall vendor health score (0–100)
• Classification — Strategic / Preferred / Review / Problematic

Click any column header to sort. Filter by classification to focus on specific vendor groups.
💡 Export this table for your vendor review meetings. Sort by "Score" ascending to find your worst performers first.

Market Trends
🎯 Purpose

Tracks how prices are changing over time across categories and vendors. Helps you understand if inflation is hitting your procurement costs, which categories are getting more expensive month-over-month, and which vendors are gradually raising their prices. It answers: "Are prices going up? By how much? In which areas?"

📊 Charts & What They Show
📈 Category Price Inflation Multi-Line
Shows Month-over-Month (MoM) price change % for top 6 categories. Each colored line = one category. Y-axis = inflation % (positive = prices went up, negative = prices went down). X-axis = time (months).

A dotted horizontal line at Y=0 separates inflation (above) from deflation (below).

Example: If the Food line is at +15% in March → Food bills were 15% more expensive in March vs February.
💡 Sudden spikes (like the 60M% spike in the chart) are data outliers — one extreme invoice that month. Focus on the steady trend lines, not the spikes.
🏪 Vendor Price Trends Multi-Line
Similar to Category Inflation but for individual vendors. Shows how your top vendors' average invoice amount has changed month by month. A rising line = vendor gradually increasing prices. A flat line = stable pricing.

3 things to watch:
📈 Steady rise = negotiate before it gets worse
📊 Flat = reliable, predictable vendor
📉 Declining = vendor may be reducing quality
💡 Use this as evidence in vendor negotiations: "Your prices have gone up 40% in 6 months — we need to discuss."
🌡️ Category Inflation Heatmap Heatmap
A grid where rows = categories, columns = last 12 months. Each cell shows the avg inflation % for that category in that month. Color coding:
🔴 Red cell = prices rose that month (inflation)
🔵 Blue/Dark cell = prices fell (deflation)
⬜ Neutral = no significant change

Spot patterns: Is Food always red in Q4? Does Travel deflate in monsoon months?
💡 Entire red rows = a category with persistent inflation problem. Time to renegotiate contracts in that category.
🔝 Highest Inflation Categories Bar Chart
Simple ranked bar chart showing average inflation % per category across the entire period. The tallest bar = the category where prices have risen the most on average. Quick summary to see which spend areas are most affected by price increases.
💡 Categories with highest average inflation should be on your priority list for contract renegotiation or supplier replacement.
🚨 All Market Alerts Alert List
All automatically generated market alerts in one place:
🔴 Price Increase Alert — Vendor X charges Y% above category average
🟠 Concentration Risk — Too many eggs in one basket (top 5 vendors = 76% spend)
🟡 Trend Warning — A category has been inflating for 3+ consecutive months

Real example from your data: "Wolfgang's Steakhouse charges 163,401% above Food & Groceries average" — this extreme alert means either one luxury catering event OR a data entry error (missing decimal point).

Forecasting & Vendor Clustering
🎯 Purpose

Uses machine learning to predict your future spend and automatically group vendors into behavior clusters. Think of it as your crystal ball for budgeting — it takes past spending patterns and forecasts what the next 6 months will look like, helping you plan budgets proactively. It answers: "How much will we spend next quarter? Which vendors behave similarly?"

📊 Charts & What They Show
🔮 Spend Forecast — Next 6 Months Forecast Line
Shows your historical spend (solid colored line = past actual data) and a forecast for the next 6 months (dashed line = prediction). Also shows a shaded confidence band around the forecast:
• Narrow band = model is confident (stable historical pattern)
• Wide band = model is uncertain (volatile/irregular historical spend)

The forecast is generated using Exponential Smoothing — a statistical method that gives more weight to recent months than older months.

How to use it: If forecast shows spend rising → increase budget allocation. If it shows decline → opportunity to reduce reserves.
💡 Important: If your historical data has extreme outliers (like the 1993–2028 date range in your dataset), the forecast may look flat or erratic. Clean data = better forecasts.
🧬 Vendor Clustering (K-Means) Scatter Plot
Uses K-Means clustering (a machine learning algorithm) to automatically group all vendors into 4 behavioral clusters based on their spend patterns and price behavior. Each dot = one vendor. Color = which cluster they belong to.

The 4 clusters typically represent:
🔵 Cluster 1 — High Volume, Low Price: Many small invoices, cheap vendors (e.g. stationery suppliers)
🟣 Cluster 2 — Low Volume, High Price: Rare but expensive invoices (e.g. consulting firms)
🟢 Cluster 3 — Balanced: Regular invoices at moderate price — your "steady" vendors
🟡 Cluster 4 — Outliers: Unusual patterns that don't fit normal behavior

X-axis = Total Spend (log scale). Y-axis = Invoice Count (log scale). Clusters are auto-detected — no manual labeling needed.
💡 Vendors in the "Outlier" cluster deserve manual review — they behave differently from all other vendors and may indicate fraud or data errors.

Anomaly Detection
🎯 Purpose

Your fraud detection and error-catching system. Uses machine learning (Isolation Forest algorithm) to automatically scan every invoice and flag ones that look statistically unusual. It answers: "Which invoices look suspicious, wrong, or potentially fraudulent?"

🤖 How it Works (Simple Explanation)

The system gives every invoice an "anomaly score". Normal invoices get a score close to 0. Suspicious invoices get a high score. Anything above the threshold is flagged as an "anomaly."

What makes an invoice anomalous? Unusually high amount, unusual day of week, vendor charging way above average, amount that doesn't match the vendor's normal pattern, etc.

📊 Charts & What They Show
📊 Anomaly Score Distribution Histogram
Shows how anomaly scores are distributed across all invoices. Most invoices cluster near 0 (normal). The few that are far to the right (high score) = the detected anomalies. The vertical red line = detection threshold. Invoices to the right of that line are flagged.
💡 A thin spike at 0 and a long tail to the right = healthy dataset with few outliers. A flat distribution = many unusual invoices.
⚖️ Anomalous vs Normal Spend Pie / Bar
Compares total money spent in normal invoices vs flagged anomalous invoices. Even if only 2% of invoices are anomalies, they might represent 30% of total spend — because anomalies are usually large-value transactions.
💡 If anomalous spend % is much higher than anomaly invoice %, it means your suspicious invoices are large-value ones — highest priority to review.
🏷️ Anomalies by Category Bar Chart
Shows which categories have the most anomalous invoices. A category with many anomalies might have loose procurement controls or a vendor gaming the system.
💡 If "Other" (uncategorized) has most anomalies, it's a sign that unclassified transactions often hide suspicious activity.
🏪 Top Vendors with Anomalies Bar Chart
Lists vendors who appear most frequently in anomalous invoices. A vendor with many anomaly flags could mean:
• They are intentionally overcharging
• Their invoices have data quality issues
• They serve an unregulated category
💡 This is your "watch list" of vendors to audit first. Cross-reference with Vendor Analytics classification.
📋 Anomalous Invoice Details Full Table
A complete table of every flagged invoice with:
• Invoice ID — unique bill identifier
• Date — when the invoice was issued
• Vendor — who billed you
• Amount — invoice value
• Category — what it was for
• Anomaly Score — how suspicious it is (higher = more suspicious)

Sorted by anomaly score (most suspicious first). Each row is a specific invoice you should physically verify with your finance team.
💡 Start from the top of this table — the highest anomaly score invoices need review first. Match them to actual receipts/purchase orders to confirm legitimacy.

Region Analytics

Breaks down procurement spending geographically — by region and country. Since your dataset doesn't have a direct "region" column, the system automatically maps each record to a region using the currency code (e.g. IDR → Southeast Asia, USD → North America, INR → South Asia). It answers: "Which part of the world is spending the most? What do different regions buy? Which regions have the fastest growing spend?"

📊 Charts & What They Show
📊 Region-wise Total Spend Horizontal Bar
Shows total spend for each region side by side. Southeast Asia and North America dominate because IDR (Indonesia) and USD are the most common currencies in the dataset. Helps identify where your biggest procurement markets are.
💡 Southeast Asia dominates because most users are Indonesian — IDR amounts, even in "Billions," are relatively normal in that currency.
🥧 Region Spend Contribution Pie / Donut
Shows each region as a percentage of total global spend. Useful for understanding your geographic procurement footprint. Is it balanced globally or concentrated in one region?
💡 If one region = 70%+ of spend, you have geographic concentration risk — similar to vendor concentration risk.
🏷️ Category Distribution by Region Horizontal Stacked Bar
For each region, shows a stacked bar broken into category colors. Each colored segment = one category's contribution to that region's total spend. Lets you compare: "Southeast Asia spends mostly on Food & Technology. Middle East spends mostly on Travel."

📈 Region Spend Trend (Monthly) Multi-Line

Grid: rows = regions, columns = categories. Each cell = spend at that region+category intersection. Dark = heavy spend. Quickly shows: "Middle East spends heavily on Travel. South Asia spends heavily on Healthcare."

🏪 Top Vendors by Region Bar Chart
For each region, shows which vendors receive the most money. A vendor dominant in one region but absent in others might be a regional contract that could be renegotiated globally for better rates.
👥 User Activity by Region Bar / Table

📌 Important Note about Region Mapping: Since your dataset doesn't have a physical "Region" column, regions are inferred from currency codes: IDR → Southeast Asia  |  USD → North America  |  INR → South Asia  |  AED → Middle East  |  NGN → West Africa, etc. Rows where currency doesn't map to a known region appear as "Global" or are excluded.
